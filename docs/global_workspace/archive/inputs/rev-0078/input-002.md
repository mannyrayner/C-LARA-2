For several years, I have been working together with a loosely federated group of humans and AIs to develop a series of platforms designed to help people build multimodal language learning resources; the paper which explains it best is probably \<a href="https\://www\.researchgate.net/publication/398798681_AI-Generated_Tailor-Made_Pedagogical_Picture_Books_How_Close_Are_We">this one\</a>. The project hasn't been a failure, but, since few people have used our tools, we can't claim it's been a success either. Evidently, something was missing.\
\
An extremely powerful AI model is now generally available in the form of OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra Extra High, and this made me wonder if it wasn't feasible to look at the radical option: just throw everything away and start again, aiming to give people what they \<i>really\</i> wanted. When I thought about the criticisms I'd received, two things stood out:\
\
1\. I'd always assumed that the primary thing was the words. You have some text in a foreign language. Then you add stuff to it, the most obvious things being glosses, translations, audio and images.\
\
2\. I'd always assumed that we'd start by developing the app to run on a laptop. When it was working well enough, it could be ported so that it also ran on a mobile device.\
\
These assumptions are natural for me, since I love reading in foreign languages and I hate mobile phones. But for most people they are very bad assumptions indeed. Images, for almost everyone in fact, are more immediate than words; also, most people use their mobile device far more than they use their laptop, if indeed they even have one. The two strands link up at the smartphone camera. Most people love taking pictures with their smartphones. \
\
So why not make \<i>that\</i> the central activity? The idea couldn't be simpler. The language learner has an app on their phone which lets them take pictures and save them to the app's library. Then they tell the app to add information to each picture, starting with a sentence describing the picture and then going on to other material: words, audio, translations, links. Here's part of a screenshot showing a typical page created by our new app, "Community Dictionaries":\
\
\<img src="https\://live.staticflickr.com/65535/55588085754_5efe8bdf7c_w\.jpg" width="392" height="500" alt="CommunityDictionariesExample"/>\
\
All the learner did was take the picture. The app then interpreted it as a cat under a chair, optionally checked with the user in English that they agreed, translated it into French, extracted the words, added audio, and linked it together with other entries. You can do it on the fly, or (in practice this turns out to be more efficient), you can accumulate a reasonable number of images and then tell the app to process all of them at once. It will sometimes make mistakes, but we find it doesn't make many mistakes, and you arrange things so that a person who knows the language can easily log in, review, and edit. \
\
Astra is phenomenally efficient, and it put the whole thing together in two and a half weeks, recycling code from our earlier projects when that looked appropriate. We've just posted an initial paper about it on ResearchGate, \<a href="https\://www\.researchgate.net/publication/415734280_Community_Dictionaries_Everyday_Pictures_Rapid_Human-AI_Development">here\</a>, and also on the new AI-based AiraXiv pre-print server, \<a href="https\://airaxiv.com/papers/view/2610.0032/">here\</a>; it gives links for logging in, browsing existing content, and creating an account. As AiraXiv's AI reviewer says, what this needs now is evaluation: if you think it's interesting and would like to try out the platform, please contact me and we'll give you some AI credits so that you can experiment. We're particularly interested in hearing from language teachers, we think there could be many ways to use this for class projects.\
\
It seems to me that modern AI is soon going to transform language learning completely. Duolingo has kept its place for many years, but as you see you now can create much more exciting and engaging things with little effort. If you can build something like our little app in two weeks with a team of one person and a late 2026 AI, what couldn't you do in six months, with a well-funded project and the even better models that we know will soon be released?
