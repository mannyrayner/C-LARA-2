# Extended Session Analysis (Session 2)

## Evidence status and source caveats

This is the English version of the AI-generated extended analysis requested at
the end of Session 2 for sharing with Branislav Bédi. The supplied source also
contained a transcript-like Voice Mode conversation export with short timing
markers. That export is not reproduced here: as with the other records in this
directory, this file preserves the post-session analysis rather than presenting
the source as a formal transcript.

The analysis is retained as generated evidence, not as an independently checked
linguistic assessment. Several examples in it—including *kein Reservierung*,
*zwei Nacht*, *Versicherung*, and *TÜV*—also occur in the Session 1 report and
are not readily identifiable in the supplied Session 2 conversation export.
The German analysis also contained a section on listening versus reading that
was omitted from the supplied English translation. These features may reflect
cross-session context reuse by ChatGPT and should be considered when using the
summary for longitudinal claims.

## 1. General impression

The session was highly interactive and communicatively successful. Sarah was
able to sustain communication throughout, even when linguistic accuracy was
limited.

**Key point:** The session clearly prioritised communication over correctness,
which the source analysis evaluates as pedagogically very positive.

## 2. Grammar

### Strengths

The analysis identifies good control of basic sentence structures:

- *Ich möchte…* (I would like…)
- *Ich bleibe eine Nacht* (I stay one night)

It also identifies an ability to form functional questions:

- *Gibt es…?* (Is there…?)
- *Wie viel kostet…?* (How much does… cost?)

### Typical errors

#### Articles and gender

- *kein Reservierung* → *keine Reservierung*
- *ein Tisch für den Laptop* → better: *einen Tisch*

The analysis characterises this as a typical issue for learners whose first
language does not have an article system.

#### Plural forms

- *zwei Nacht* → *zwei Nächte*

The analysis interprets this as plural marking not yet being automatised.

#### Word order

The analysis notes occasional inconsistency caused by mixing English and German
syntactic patterns.

#### Modality and register

- *Ich will…* instead of *Ich möchte…*

This is described as communicatively correct but not always pragmatically
appropriate.

## 3. Vocabulary

### Strengths

The analysis identifies strong everyday communicative vocabulary and effective
use of:

- travel-related terms;
- food vocabulary; and
- real-life interaction language.

### Gaps

#### Domain-specific vocabulary

The following items are described as missing or unstable:

- *Stellplatz* (camping pitch);
- *Versicherung* (insurance); and
- *TÜV* (vehicle inspection).

#### Use of circumlocution

The analysis gives "place for car and two people" instead of *Stellplatz für
zwei Personen* as an example. It observes that communication remained
successful despite the lexical gap.

## 4. Error-handling strategies

The analysis identifies the following strategies as relevant from a research
perspective:

- **Code-switching:** German to English.
- **Approximation:** simplified structures used to maintain communication.
- **Self-repair:** reformulation in the middle of an utterance.

It evaluates these as advanced communicative strategies rather than
deficiencies, and as evidence of active communicative competence.

## 5. Multilingual influence

The English analysis says that Sarah was working across German, Dutch, and
English. It identifies rapid semantic inference and high flexibility as
positive effects, and phonological and syntactic interference as negative
effects. Overall, it presents multilingualism as a net facilitator rather than
a barrier.

The German analysis supplied immediately before the English version also listed
Swedish; the difference is preserved here as a source inconsistency rather than
silently resolved.

## 6. Fluency and accuracy profile

| Dimension | Level |
|---|---|
| Fluency | Relatively high |
| Accuracy | Moderate |
| Comprehension | Good with slowed input |

The analysis characterises this as a familiar profile: communicatively strong
and structurally developing.

## 7. Affective factors

The analysis highlights:

- high motivation connected with travel goals;
- strong personal relevance through culture and real-life situations; and
- emotional engagement during discussion of the Iran conflict.

It concludes that these factors made the language use meaningful and authentic.

## 8. Pedagogical implications

### What worked well

- roleplay-based interaction;
- real-world scenarios; and
- flexible topic shifts.

### What should be developed

- targeted listening practice;
- repetition of key structures; and
- light grammar reinforcement without overload, especially for plural forms.

## 9. Recommendations for the next session

1. **Travel planning (Scenario 7):** describe routes and explain decisions.
2. **Medical or emergency situations (Scenario 9):** describe symptoms and ask
   for help.
3. Discuss the culture and cuisine of different countries.

## 10. Final conclusion

The source analysis concludes that Sarah was already functioning as a
real-world communicator. It identifies structural stabilisation and development
of listening comprehension, rather than vocabulary acquisition alone, as the
main areas for further development.

## 11. Research potential

The analysis presents the session as evidence for AI-led roleplay as a tool for
real-world language preparation. It suggests the following possible framing:

- task-based interaction;
- multilingual learners; and
- low-anxiety AI interaction environments.

It proposes either developing the material into a EuroCALL-style paper draft or
comparing multiple sessions longitudinally.
