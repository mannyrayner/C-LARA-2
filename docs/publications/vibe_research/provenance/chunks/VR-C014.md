**AI**

Yes, all seven look good to me. I’d make the changes pretty much exactly as you suggest, with one qualification on 6.

1. For the transition, I prefer your formulation. I’d use:

> But if the technical analogy is as close as we suggest, the original question remains. Why have the two communities developed such different norms?

That keeps the paper visibly anchored to its central puzzle.

2. Agreed: remove “entirely.” It weakens a point we actually have evidence for. I’d rewrite the end of §5 as:

> This provides a concrete reason for expecting high-end software agents to be useful research agents as well. No new architecture is required. Modern software assistants are already designed to work with code, experimental outputs, documentation, publications, and other structured project materials. The same system which can inspect a software repository can inspect research materials stored inside it.
>
> Indeed, when a software repository is being used in support of a research project, it is entirely natural, other things being equal, to store the associated papers, prompts, experimental materials, results, analysis scripts, and research documentation in the same repository. The repository then becomes not simply a container for software but a structured memory for the research project as a whole.
>
> The next section shows what this looks like in practice.

That last sentence gives us the forward transition you want.

3–5. Yes, definitely add section numbers on repeated citations. Once we’re using a paper as evidence for a fairly specific claim, `\citep[Section~X]{...}` is much better than repeatedly citing the whole item. I’d want to verify the exact section numbering in all three papers before inserting them, rather than guess.

6. I would **not quite say “the largest” unless we have a reliable quantitative basis for that exact superlative**. ACL’s own materials call it “the premier international scientific and professional society” and elsewhere “the most authoritative and influential international academic organization” in computational linguistics and NLP. ([ACL 2023][1])

So I’d use something like:

> The ACL Policy on Publication Ethics provides a particularly instructive example because ACL is the premier international scientific and professional society for computational linguistics and natural-language processing.

That is strong, sourced in ACL’s own wording, and avoids an unnecessary factual vulnerability.

7. Yes. I think that addition gets at something central about why software-agent infrastructure transfers so naturally to research. Perhaps:

> Seen in these terms, the technical problem posed by AI collaboration is less exotic than it initially appears. Serious software projects already need persistent project memory, versioned artefacts, tests, documentation, issue histories, repository-grounded explanation, and flexible natural-language mechanisms for recording, accessing, and discussing project information.

I might even slightly strengthen “mechanisms” to “interfaces”:

> ...and flexible natural-language interfaces for recording, accessing, and discussing project information.

That feels closer to the actual agentic workflow.

The only remaining mechanical job before the Codex handoff, then, is to verify the section numbers for the three example papers and insert them. Everything else here is straightforward prose tightening.
