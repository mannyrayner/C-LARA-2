## Summary

Newton interviews Shipper about Every, a company combining technology journalism with an AI-oriented product studio. Shipper describes an organisation in which AI performs essentially all software implementation while human authors still mostly write its journalism. He argues that automation has moved engineering work to a higher level rather than eliminating it and says that Every expanded its staff while automating more work.

A central example concerns editor-in-chief Kate Lee. Every assembled a dataset of approximately 30,000 historical edits, used it to develop and back-test an editing agent, and deployed the agent to make aspects of Lee's editorial judgement available across the organisation. Shipper presents this as a way to distribute an expert's limited capacity, not simply replace the expert. He also compares accepting AI-generated prose changes with accepting a human editor's changes and describes norms around AI-supported writing as unsettled.

## Relevance to *Vibe Research*

The research discussion identifies four uses for the article:

1. Every provides a within-organisation example of the contrast between extensive accepted AI delegation in software and more sensitive norms around signed prose.
2. Shipper's account of engineering having “moved up a level” supports the paper's description of human work shifting toward supervision and judgement.
3. The Kate Lee editing agent illustrates expertise being externalised, distributed, and made reusable.
4. Shipper's comments about undisclosed AI use in professional writing suggest a disclosure problem, but the session explicitly treats this as anecdotal rather than population-level evidence.
